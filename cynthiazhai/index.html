Observation One

Your Reels get 2x the engagement of your images - but half your content is still static.

Across 100 posts analyzed, your Reels average 8.0 likes per post versus 3.7 for single images. That is not a small gap. It is the difference between content that reaches and content that stays in place. And yet 50 of your last 100 posts were static images - the format that performs worst for your account specifically.

Data: 31 Reels (avg 8.0L, 98.7 views) vs 50 Images (avg 3.7L) - from 100-post audit, April 2026. Your Reels have reach potential; your images largely do not.

Observation Two

The same myth has been written five times in the last six months. The audience has stopped seeing it.

The "loud does not equal powerful" angle is your most familiar territory - and it appears in at least five separate posts with nearly identical structure, logic, and bullet points. This is not laziness; it is a topic you genuinely believe in. But when a cold reader sees the same insight framed the same way repeatedly, the algorithm learns to show it to fewer people, and the existing audience learns to scroll past.

Posts reviewed: DVfpefOibvC, DWF0j0nCZsQ, DWIQetYjVOP, DWDJSm-Cfjs, DWaSB0qEe1w - all published within 12 weeks, all covering the same core myth. The opportunity is not to stop believing it - it is to say it differently.

Observation Three

Your biggest differentiator - coaching in a language that is not your first - is almost invisible on your feed.

You once wrote: "English is my 2nd language, yet I've made a great living speaking and coaching primarily in English." That single fact is worth more than any credential. For the enormous audience of non-native English speakers in Asia who want to be heard in professional environments, you are not just a voice coach - you are living proof. But in the last 100 posts, this angle appeared in maybe one or two sentences. It deserves its own series.

Post DVfpefOibvC (Mar 2026, 6L) briefly mentions this. Your top engagement post by a significant margin was a Chinese New Year Reel (45L, 878 views) - the one moment your Asian identity visibly entered the feed. The audience is there; the content is not meeting them.

Sample Carousel Script - "The Thing I Was Told Would Hold Me Back"

Slide 01 - Hook

When I first started as a voice coach, someone told me my English would limit my career. English, after all, was not my first language. I thought about that comment for a long time.

Slide 02

At 6 years old, I failed an entrance exam because my sister left the room and I panicked. At 17, I had never imagined I would one day coach others on how to speak. At the start of my career, I sat in meetings feeling unheard. Not because of my ideas. Because my voice did not yet carry the weight of my conviction.

Slide 03

What changed was not my English. What changed was my voice. When I learned to breathe properly, to speak from my body rather than from my throat, to let resonance do the work that volume cannot - the language became secondary. Presence is not a mother tongue.

Slide 04

Today, many of my clients are non-native English speakers. Leaders from Singapore, Malaysia, India, China, working in environments where English is expected - and where a voice that sounds uncertain can cost you the room. They do not come to me to improve their grammar. They come to me because they want their voice to carry the same weight as their ideas. That gap - between what you know and how you land - is exactly what voice coaching closes.

Slide 05

The truth that took me 16 years to fully understand: The professionals who command rooms are not always the most articulate. They are not always the most senior. They are the ones whose voice arrives in the room before they finish their sentence. That quality is trainable. I know. Because I trained it in myself first.

Slide 06 - Close

If you are a non-native English speaker working in a professional environment - and you have ever felt that the barrier was the language when it was actually the voice - you are not alone. And it is not a permanent condition. Your voice is an instrument. It can be developed. I have spent 16 years proving that. Starting with my own. -- What is the one moment you realized your voice was not matching the quality of your ideas? Share below.
